1Why spring is the window

Perth's warm season lawns (couch, kikuyu, buffalo) do almost all of their recovery growth between September and April. Renovate in spring and your lawn has the whole season to thicken up before summer heat arrives. Wait until summer and you're asking a stressed lawn to repair itself in the worst conditions of the year.

2Five signs your lawn needs it

1It feels spongy underfoot. That's thatch, a dead layer of runners choking the surface.
2Water sits on top instead of soaking in. Compacted, hydrophobic soil needs aeration.
3Yellow or bare patches that don't green up. Roots aren't reaching air, water or nutrients.
4It's been more than 2 years since a proper renovation. Thatch rebuilds every season.
5Your water bill is climbing but the lawn still looks thirsty. The water isn't reaching the roots.

3What actually happens on the day

Dethatch

Vertical blades strip out the dead runner layer choking your lawn, the single biggest reset a tired lawn can get. It will look scalped for a week or two, that's normal.

Aerate

Coring relieves compaction and opens the soil profile so water and nutrients reach the roots instead of running off the surface.

Top dress

A levelling layer of soil goes down over the aerated surface, improving structure and giving new growth the best possible start.

5Your recovery timeline

Days 1 to 7: water deeply, twice a day, don't panic at the scalped look. Week 2: feed it, fresh green shoots fill the gaps. Weeks 2 to 3: first mow, high setting only. Week 4 onward: back to normal, keep a wetting agent going through summer.
